# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Retail-curious beginners who want to understand stock and options trading mechanics
without risking money or wading through jargon. They arrive with little-to-no formal
finance background, often on a laptop in a self-directed learning session. The job to be
done: build genuine intuition for charts, the order book, short selling, options, and
volatility through short, hands-on, interactive modules — not passive reading.

## Product Purpose

A Brilliant.org-style interactive course for trading literacy. Five lessons (84 modules)
teach via animated, manipulable Phaser scenes backed by real, verified market data and
deterministic, exactly-correct math. Success looks like: a learner completes a lesson,
can reason about the concept afterward, returns to continue (streak + per-lesson resume),
and trusts that what they were shown is accurate.

## Brand Personality

Clear, confident, encouraging. The voice of a great teacher: it makes an intimidating
domain feel approachable without dumbing it down. Three words: lucid, trustworthy, warm.
Emotional goal — the learner should feel capable and curious, never overwhelmed or sold to.

## Anti-references

- An intimidating, jargon-heavy trading terminal (dense tickers, blinking numbers, walls
  of data with no teaching scaffolding).
- A generic, cookie-cutter SaaS dashboard / template look (identical card grids, hero-metric
  blocks, decorative gradient chrome that says nothing).
- Hype / gambling / "get rich quick" energy.

## Design Principles

- **Show, don't tell.** Concepts are taught by manipulating a live scene, not by paragraphs.
- **Earned trust through accuracy.** Real data and correct math; illustrative figures are
  labelled as such. Never imply precision the content doesn't have.
- **One idea per module.** Each screen has a single focus and a single clear next action.
- **Calm confidence over hype.** Restraint in color and motion; the content is the spectacle.
- **The tool disappears into the task.** Familiar, predictable chrome; surprise is reserved
  for the teaching moments inside the canvas.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target committed (best-effort). Even so, the design should respect the
shared craft floor: legible body contrast, semantic color that doesn't rely on hue alone
(green-up/red-down is reinforced with labels/shape), keyboard-reachable controls, and a
reduced-motion fallback where motion is used.
