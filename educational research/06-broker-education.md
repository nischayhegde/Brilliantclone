# Brokerage-Provided Trading Education: Competitive Research

_Research brief for a standalone trading/stock/options **education** app. Current as of 2025–2026._

---

## Introduction

Nearly every U.S. retail brokerage now runs a substantial "learning center." Education is no longer a nice-to-have add-on; it has become a **primary customer-acquisition and retention channel**. Brokers publish free articles, videos, structured courses, live webinars, in-app lessons, and one-on-one coaching, and they tie that content directly to account opening and trade execution.

For a team building a **standalone education app**, the broker landscape is both the main competitive set and the main reference point. The key strategic insight is that broker education is almost always **free but not neutral**: it is designed to move a user from "curious" to "funded account" to "active trader." This creates an opening for an independent, product-agnostic educational product that optimizes for genuine learner mastery rather than trade volume.

This document profiles the major brokerage education hubs, compares them, and distills the trends and opportunities most relevant to an independent education product.

---

## How to read each profile

For each brokerage we capture: the education product/URL, the **type of content**, **coverage** (options vs. stocks vs. general investing), **cost/gating**, **quality & reputation**, **how it funnels users**, and **why beginners use it**.

## Brokerage Education Profiles

### 1. Charles Schwab — thinkorswim Learning Center + Schwab Coaching

- **Products / URLs:** Schwab Learn (`schwab.com/learn`), thinkorswim Learning Center (`toslc.thinkorswim.com/center`), Schwab Coaching (`schwab.com/coaching`), thinkorswim Tutorials (`schwab.com/learn/thinkorswim-demos`). Now branded **"Schwab Trading Powered by Ameritrade"** after the TD Ameritrade integration.
- **Type of content:** Articles/stories, platform tutorial videos, thinkScript scripting docs, **30+ free live interactive webcasts per week**, virtual workshops, in-person events, on-demand webcast library (24/7), and a large YouTube presence. Coaches (e.g., Cameron May / "Coach Cam") host recurring series.
- **Coverage:** Very broad — general investing, technical and fundamental analysis, whole-portfolio management, **and a deep options track**. Dedicated "Getting Started with Options," "Getting Started with Technical Analysis," "Getting Started with Investing," and "Getting Started with thinkorswim" beginner series, plus intermediate/advanced strategy webcasts.
- **Cost / gating:** Free. Live coaching and the Learning Center are open; the deepest value (thinkorswim platform, tailored education, specialized support) assumes you're a Schwab/thinkorswim customer.
- **Quality & reputation:** Considered among the strongest in the industry, inherited from TD Ameritrade's renowned education legacy. Calendar filters (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced) make it navigable. Live, interactive Q&A with coaches is a standout differentiator.
- **How it funnels users:** Education → familiarity/comfort with the thinkorswim platform → funded Schwab account → active (often options) trader. The webcasts repeatedly demonstrate concepts "on Schwab platforms with live examples."
- **Why beginners use it:** Huge volume of free, structured, live human instruction; the ability to ask questions in real time; and a clear beginner→advanced ladder.

### 2. Fidelity — Learning Center + Viewpoints + Trading Strategy Desk

- **Products / URLs:** Fidelity Learning Center (`fidelity.com/learning-center`), Options education hub (`fidelity.com/options-trading/education`), Fidelity Viewpoints (market commentary), and the **Trading Strategy Desk®** (live coaching + classes).
- **Type of content:** Articles, videos, beginner series, on-demand webinars, a **4-week virtual options classroom course**, small-group live classes, 1:1 coaching sessions, and interactive tools (Options Strategy Builder, Probability Calculator, P/L calculator, Strategy Evaluator).
- **Coverage:** Comprehensive general investing + retirement/planning, strong **options** track ("Options for beginners," buying vs. selling strategies, pricing, Greeks, trade management), and stock research education.
- **Cost / gating:** Free content; full tool suite and options trading require an account + options approval (application/approval gating is a natural funnel step).
- **Quality & reputation:** Frequently ranked **#1 or top-tier for education** (e.g., StockBrokers.com praises education being woven into the experience rather than hidden; CNBC Select repeatedly cites Fidelity for customer service and beginner-friendliness). Clear, jargon-light writing.
- **How it funnels users:** "Learn → tools → apply for options → place first trade." The education explicitly bridges to the Options Strategy Builder and to opening/funding an account.
- **Why beginners use it:** Trusted brand, plain-English explanations, structured "first options trade" pathway, and tools that turn lessons into action.

### 3. tastylive / tastytrade — Education-first broker

- **Products / URLs:** tastylive learn portal (`learn.tastylive.com`), free courses (`tastylive.com/learn-courses`), daily live programming, and the broker **tastytrade** (`tastytrade.com`, formerly tastyworks; a wholly-owned subsidiary of tastylive). Now part of **IG Group**.
- **Type of content:** Free video courses (e.g., 40-lesson Beginner Options Course), daily live financial programming/shows ("Options Trading Concepts LIVE," "Mike and his Whiteboard," "Options Crash Course: Stocks to Options"), cheat sheets (Options Strategies Cheat Sheet), and an enormous on-demand archive.
- **Coverage:** **Options-centric** (the brand's identity), plus futures and stock/ETF fundamentals framed as a "step up to options." Heavy emphasis on premium-selling, implied volatility, and probability-based strategies.
- **Cost / gating:** Education is free and largely **ungated** (you don't need a tastytrade account to watch). Brokerage is low-cost/active-trader oriented.
- **Quality & reputation:** Distinctive, opinionated, personality-driven; tastytrade wins "best options platform" awards (2025). The content has a strong house view (sell premium, manage at high probability), which is engaging but not neutral.
- **How it funnels users:** The media business **is** the acquisition engine — content drives brokerage sign-ups and lowers CAC. IG Group's strategy explicitly uses tastytrade media as a content-driven direct-to-consumer channel ("Options Reimagined" campaign).
- **Why beginners use it:** Entertaining, daily, free, and unusually deep on options for a retail audience; a clear community/personality hook.

### 4. Webull — Webull Learn + paper trading

- **Products / URLs:** Webull Learn / Learn Hub (in-app + web), plus an industry-leading **paper trading simulator**.
- **Type of content:** 500+ video courses/modules, how-to platform tutorials, articles, term glossaries, and **paper trading** with live market data (stocks, ETFs, options, futures). Some basic educational material sourced from CME Group and S&P Dow Jones Indices.
- **Coverage:** Trading strategies, instruments, technical analysis, platform tooling; options and stocks both covered. Strength is **"learn by doing."**
- **Cost / gating:** Free; paper trading does not require a funded account.
- **Quality & reputation:** Won StockBrokers.com **#1 Paper Trading**. Written content is broad but reviewers call it denser, "oddly sequenced," and thinner on depth than Robinhood's. Education ranks mid-pack (~#10), but the **simulator is the differentiator**.
- **How it funnels users:** Simulator + data-rich tools build confidence and habit → conversion to live, active trading (Webull skews toward more active/technical traders).
- **Why beginners use it:** Risk-free practice with real-time data, advanced charting, and a large catalog of bite-size video lessons.

### 5. Robinhood — Robinhood Learn

- **Products / URLs:** Robinhood Learn (`robinhood.com/learn`), plus Sherwood News / "Snacks" newsletter, Chartr data insights, a podcast, and a YouTube library.
- **Type of content:** Beginner-friendly **articles and guides** (Investing 101, options articles), news, newsletter, and video. **No formal courses, no financial calculators, no native paper trading.**
- **Coverage:** General investing fundamentals + a well-regarded options article set. Breadth is good; depth is intentionally limited.
- **Cost / gating:** Free and open.
- **Quality & reputation:** Widely cited as having the **clearest, most readable writing of any broker** — ordered so a beginner moves idea-to-idea without getting lost. Conversely, Robinhood is the poster child for **gamification controversy** (see Trends): confetti, lottery-style rewards, push notifications, curated popular-stock lists. Massachusetts charged Robinhood over gamified design; it **settled (~$7.5M)** and removed contested features.
- **How it funnels users:** Minimalist app + frictionless onboarding + simple, confidence-building content → fast first trade. The design lowers psychological barriers (a double-edged sword regulators scrutinize).
- **Why beginners use it:** Easiest on-ramp, simplest UI, and the most approachable prose; great for absolute first-timers who "learn by reading."

### 6. E*TRADE (Morgan Stanley) — Knowledge Center + Options webinars

- **Products / URLs:** E*TRADE Knowledge Center (`us.etrade.com/knowledge`), Options library (`/knowledge/library/options`), Events (`/knowledge/events`), and Morgan Stanley thought leadership.
- **Type of content:** Articles, videos, podcasts, and a **deep webinar program**: Options Summit, **Options Boot Camp (6-webinar series)**, Options Forum (6 advanced sessions), platform orientation (Power E*TRADE), plus Morgan Stanley research/market insights and recurring "Getting Started with Options" sessions.
- **Coverage:** Strong general investing + retirement/planning, **robust options education** (basics, covered calls, Greeks, 0DTE, spreads), and stock research. Institutional-grade Morgan Stanley insights are a post-acquisition differentiator.
- **Cost / gating:** Free; deeper tools (Power E*TRADE) and trading require an account/options approval.
- **Quality & reputation:** CNBC Select names E*TRADE **"Best for educational resources" (2026)**. Comprehensive, well-produced, with institutional research access.
- **How it funnels users:** Boot Camp / Summit → Power E*TRADE platform orientation → funded account → options trading. Morgan Stanley content adds credibility and cross-sell into wealth.
- **Why beginners use it:** Structured "boot camp" format with a clear curriculum, plus the prestige of Morgan Stanley research.

### 7. Interactive Brokers — Traders' Academy / IBKR Campus

- **Products / URLs:** IBKR Campus (`interactivebrokers.com/campus`), Traders' Academy (`/campus/traders-academy`), with dedicated Stocks and Options tracks and free options education hub.
- **Type of content:** **Curriculum-style courses** with lessons, notes, and **quizzes**; instructor-led video courses; live webinars; podcasts; and "how-to" platform tutorials (TWS, IBKR Mobile, GlobalTrader, PortfolioAnalyst, Probability Lab). Open to clients **and the general public.**
- **Coverage:** Extremely broad — stocks, indexes, ETFs/ETPs, value/growth/momentum investing, dividends, short selling, margin, fixed income, futures, forex, and a **deep options program** (calls/puts, Greeks, valuation, basic→advanced strategies, volatility tools).
- **Cost / gating:** Free; no account required for most courses.
- **Quality & reputation:** Reviewers praise it as the most **structured/sequenced, curriculum-like** learning environment — "lessons that build logically on one another." Information-dense; best suited to engaged/serious learners.
- **How it funnels users:** Builds genuine competence + platform familiarity (TWS) → conversion to IBKR's pro-grade brokerage. Also positions IBKR as a thought leader (e.g., ARK Invest disruptive-innovation content).
- **Why beginners use it:** Free, rigorous, sequenced curriculum with quizzes — closest of the brokers to a "real course."

### 8. Merrill Edge (Bank of America) — Investor Education + Morningstar Classroom

- **Products / URLs:** Merrill investor education (`merrilledge.com/investor-education`), Investing 101, "How to" video series, and Bank of America's **Better Money Habits**.
- **Type of content:** Articles, how-to videos, webinars, and the **Morningstar Classroom** — slideshow courses that "graduate from basic to advanced" with **quizzes**. Heavy on guidance/retirement and portfolio construction.
- **Coverage:** General investing, portfolio building, asset allocation, retirement, plus stocks/ETFs/options at an introductory-to-intermediate level (multi-leg options for advanced users). Less day-trading-oriented than Schwab/IBKR.
- **Cost / gating:** Free; integrated with BofA banking ecosystem.
- **Quality & reputation:** Solid, well-rounded, leverages Morningstar and BofA brand trust. Praised for offering the same topic as article, video, **or** webinar (multiple learning styles). More planning-centric than trading-centric.
- **How it funnels users:** Education + BofA banking relationship → Merrill self-directed or **Merrill Guided Investing** (advisory) → deeper wealth relationship. Strong cross-sell from banking.
- **Why beginners use it:** Trusted bank brand, gentle hand-holding, and a planning/retirement orientation that suits cautious newcomers.

### 9. Public.com — Community/social education

- **Products / URLs:** Public Learn (`public.com/learn`), Public Live (in-app expert commentary), social feed, and Public Premium (analyst insights).
- **Type of content:** Beginner articles/guides, **social/community learning** (discuss strategies, follow other investors), and live audio/commentary from professionals. Education is interwoven with real market discussion.
- **Coverage:** General investing basics, "what are stocks," beginner strategy; options and a multi-asset lineup exist, but the hallmark is **community-driven** learning rather than a formal curriculum.
- **Cost / gating:** Core content free; **Public Premium** (analyst insights) is paid/gated.
- **Quality & reputation:** Regarded as a strong **social-learning** option; good for learners who like crowd insight and transparency. Less deep/structured than Fidelity/IBKR.
- **How it funnels users:** Social proof + community + fractional shares ($1 min) lower the barrier → account funding → Premium upsell.
- **Why beginners use it:** Learn alongside peers, ask the community, and start tiny with fractional shares.

### 10. SoFi — SoFi Learn / Investing 101 Center

- **Products / URLs:** SoFi Learn & **Investing 101 Center** (`sofi.com/investing-101-center`).
- **Type of content:** Articles, guides, short explainer videos ("How to Trade Stocks for Beginners in under 4 minutes"), glossaries, and goal-setting content. Part of an all-in-one personal-finance ecosystem.
- **Coverage:** Broad **personal finance + investing basics**, retirement, goal setting; lighter on advanced options/active trading. Self-directed and automated (robo) investing both supported.
- **Cost / gating:** Free; fractional shares from $5; ties to SoFi banking/lending ecosystem.
- **Quality & reputation:** Approachable and beginner-oriented; strongest as part of a **financial super-app** (banking, loans, invest) rather than as a trading-depth resource.
- **How it funnels users:** Education + direct-deposit/banking relationship → SoFi Invest (active or automated) → cross-sell across the ecosystem (and access to financial planners with direct deposit).
- **Why beginners use it:** One app for money + investing; gentle, lifestyle-oriented education for true novices.

### 11. moomoo — moomoo Learn + paper trading + community

- **Products / URLs:** moomoo Learn (in-app + web), Options Quickstart Guide course, options hub (`moomoo.com/us/invest/options`), and **paper trading** (`moomoo.com/us/papertrading`).
- **Type of content:** **2,000+ educational resources/courses** (built for all levels), video tutorials, webinars, an interactive "Moo Community," paper-trading competitions, and a robust **simulator** ($1M virtual for stocks/options, $10M for futures) using live data.
- **Coverage:** Stocks, options (dedicated multi-section options course covering the four elements, pricing, exercise vs. closing, basic strategies, volatility), and futures. Strong "learn → practice" loop.
- **Cost / gating:** Free; paper trading requires no funded brokerage account.
- **Quality & reputation:** Increasingly competitive on **volume of content + simulator quality**; data-rich, community-driven. Comparable positioning to Webull (practice-first), aimed at engaged retail traders.
- **How it funnels users:** Free courses + paper trading + community + competitions → habit formation → funded, active trading account.
- **Why beginners use it:** Big free course library, realistic risk-free practice, gamified competitions, and an active community.

## Key Trends & Takeaways

1. **Education as customer acquisition (the dominant model).** Brokers increasingly treat free education as the cheapest, highest-trust acquisition channel. The canonical case study is **Zerodha's "Varsity"** (India), which helped scale to 10M+ users with effectively **zero ad spend** — product-agnostic, free, structured content that lowered CAC, raised trust, and produced higher-quality (longer-retained, more responsible) customers. Western analogs: **IG Group acquiring tastytrade** specifically for its content-driven D2C funnel; **NerdWallet/Investopedia** building audiences by teaching first; **Public/Betterment** using explainer content. Education that ranks organically **compounds** and reduces paid-acquisition dependence over time.

2. **The "learn → then trade" funnel is now explicit.** Content is architected as a funnel: top-of-funnel SEO explainers (what are options?) → middle-of-funnel comparisons/calculators/quizzes → bottom-of-funnel onboarding (open account, get options approval, place first trade) → retention via in-app nudges, newsletters, and webinars. Fidelity's "learn → tools → apply for options → trade" and E*TRADE's "Boot Camp → Power E*TRADE" are textbook examples.

3. **In-app / embedded learning and "learn by doing."** The strongest platforms **embed** education into the trading experience rather than siloing it (StockBrokers.com explicitly praises Fidelity for this). **Paper-trading simulators** (Webull #1, moomoo's $1M virtual, Schwab's paperMoney in thinkorswim) are the clearest expression — practice with live data, no capital at risk, often **no funded account required**. moomoo adds **paper-trading competitions** for engagement.

4. **Live + human instruction as a moat.** Schwab Coaching (30+ live webcasts/week with interactive Q&A), Fidelity's Trading Strategy Desk classes/coaching, E*TRADE's webinar series, and tastylive's daily live programming show that **live, personality-driven, interactive** content is a major retention/differentiation lever that static articles can't match.

5. **Gamification — engagement vs. harm.** Robinhood pioneered gamified design (confetti, lottery-style rewards, push notifications, curated popular-stock lists, leaderboard/streak mechanics). Regulators (SEC "Digital Engagement Practices" RFI; FINRA scrutiny; **Massachusetts** action that Robinhood settled for ~$7.5M and removed features) warn these "dark patterns" can drive impulsive, excessive trading **against** investor interest, especially under the PFOF/zero-commission model that rewards engagement. **Lesson for an education app:** gamification (streaks, badges, quizzes, progress) is powerful for *learning motivation* but should be aimed at **mastery and good habits**, not trade frequency — the opposite incentive from a PFOF broker.

6. **Quality is uneven and structure is the differentiator.** Reviewers consistently reward **sequenced, curriculum-like** learning (IBKR Traders' Academy, Merrill's Morningstar Classroom, Fidelity) and ding broker libraries that are broad but **poorly organized** with "no guided learning paths" (a common critique of Webull and even Schwab's "The Know"/Investing Insights). Clear writing (Robinhood) and learning-by-doing (Webull/moomoo) are the other axes of quality.

7. **Free but not neutral — the core opening for an independent app.** Broker education is structurally **sales-linked**: success is measured by conversion (funded accounts, trades, options approvals, advisory upsells), and house views creep in (e.g., tastylive's premium-selling bias; brokers steering toward their own tools/products). Independent/neutral education ("agency-first," product-agnostic) is increasingly valued precisely because it has **no embedded sales funnel**.

### What a standalone education app can do better / differently

- **True neutrality & trust.** No brokerage P&L tied to trade volume means the curriculum can teach **risk honestly** (e.g., "most active traders underperform," position sizing, why options sellers blow up) without conflicting incentives. This is a credibility moat brokers can't fully claim.
- **Mastery-oriented pedagogy, not conversion.** Structured, sequenced learning paths with **spaced repetition, quizzes, prerequisites, and demonstrated competency** — the Brilliant.org / Duolingo model applied to markets — beats the scattered article libraries most brokers ship.
- **Healthy gamification.** Streaks, XP, levels, and badges tuned to **learning consistency and good-habit formation** rather than trade frequency — sidestepping the regulatory/ethical trap brokers fell into.
- **Broker-agnostic + interoperable.** Teach concepts that apply across platforms; optionally integrate **paper trading / simulated execution** so users practice without picking a broker first. Can even be a neutral "front door" that brokers *want* well-informed users coming from.
- **Personalization & assessment.** Diagnose a learner's level, adapt the path, and certify competency — something broker hubs (with calendar-filtered webcasts and unsorted article piles) rarely do well.
- **Depth on options without the bias.** Options are the highest-intent, highest-confusion topic and where brokers funnel hardest. A rigorous, unbiased options curriculum (Greeks, pricing, strategy trade-offs, assignment, risk) is a strong wedge.
- **Monetization aligned with the learner.** Subscription/B2B/licensing revenue (vs. PFOF/commissions) keeps incentives pointed at learner outcomes — the same structural advantage that made Zerodha Varsity trusted, applied as the *primary* business rather than a loss leader.
