# Podcasts & Newsletters That Beginner Traders Use to Learn (2025–2026)

> Research brief for the product team building a trading / stock / options **education** app.
> Goal: understand the audio (podcast) and email (newsletter) ecosystem that amateur and beginner traders rely on to learn the craft and stay current — and extract trends an education app can exploit to build daily learning habits.
> Compiled: June 2026. All facts cross-checked against live web sources (see **Sources**).

---

## 1. Why This Matters

Most beginner traders do **not** start their journey inside a structured course. They start with *ambient, low-commitment media* — a podcast on the commute and a newsletter in the inbox each morning. These two formats are the top of the funnel for self-directed financial learning:

- A 2025–2026 fintech consumer study found **81% of consumers are actively seeking financial education**, and when asked where they turn, they cite **podcasts (27%)** and **financial news websites/newsletters (27%)** ahead of their own banking/brokerage apps (only 19%). Friends/family, professionals, and social-media influencers rank above apps too.
- This is the strategic gap our app can fill: people want to learn, they're consuming podcasts + newsletters to do it, but the apps they already use aren't teaching them.

Two big behavioral truths fall out of this:

1. **Audio = passive, habit-forming, scalable.** Podcasts ride existing routines (commute, gym, chores). They build *familiarity and motivation* more than hard skill.
2. **Daily-digest newsletters = the habit anchor.** A 3–5 minute morning email trains a daily "open → read → feel informed" loop. That loop is exactly the behavior an education app wants to own.

## 2. Podcasts

Podcasts split into roughly four buckets for beginners:

- **Trader-interview / mindset** (Chat With Traders, Trading Nut, Desire To Trade, Top Traders Unplugged) — *aspiration, psychology, "how pros think."*
- **Options/strategy education** (tastylive shows, Option Alpha) — *mechanics, probability, risk management.*
- **Value/long-term investing** (We Study Billionaires, InvestED, Motley Fool Money) — *fundamentals, buy-and-hold philosophy.*
- **Market commentary / "edutainment"** (Animal Spirits, The Compound & Friends, Odd Lots) — *staying current, context, personality-driven.*

### 2.2 Notes on standout shows

- **Chat With Traders** — One of the original trading podcasts (since 2015) and still a beginner favorite. Reviews repeatedly call it an "invaluable resource for an aspiring trader." It teaches *how* successful people approach markets (discipline, risk, psychology) rather than giving signals. Disclaimer-forward ("not financial advice").
- **tastylive** — The media arm of the tastytrade brokerage. The free **Beginner Options Course** (40 lessons) plus the *Mike & His Whiteboard* series are widely cited as among the best free options on-ramps. Caveat from reviewers: tastylive has *huge* volume but **no structured curriculum**, so pure beginners can feel lost — it shines once you know the basics.
- **Option Alpha** — Founder Kirk Du Plessis. The podcast **rebooted in late 2025** with a planned 6–8 episode "mini-series" on core concepts (probabilities, trade management, position sizing) plus trader interviews and case studies. Heavy current emphasis on **0DTE options** and **automation/bots** — a signal of where retail options culture is heading.
- **We Study Billionaires** — Self-described largest stock-investing podcast in the world (180M+ downloads). Spawns sub-series (Richer Wiser Happier; Bitcoin Fundamentals) and a **free daily newsletter** — a model of audio → email cross-pollination.
- **Animal Spirits / The Compound & Friends** — Both from **Ritholtz Wealth Management's** media operation (The Compound). This is the clearest example of the **"finance creator flywheel"**: free podcasts + YouTube + newsletter + the *What Are Your Thoughts* show, all feeding a brand. Tone is casual, funny, human — the template for "edutainment."
- **Odd Lots** — Bloomberg's hit show. Not a how-to-trade podcast, but it models how to make complex market topics *genuinely entertaining* — a useful tonal reference for our app's "explainer" content.
- **Reputational caution: Trading Nut** — Interesting interview catalog (forex/futures/prop-firm traders), but third-party reviews flag a weak website, little independent buzz, and pervasive affiliate links. Fine as one input, not a quality benchmark.

## 3. Newsletters

Newsletters split into three beginner-relevant buckets:

- **Free daily digests** (Morning Brew, The Daily Upside, Robinhood Snacks/Sherwood, Finimize, 1440, Axios Markets) — *habit-forming "5-minute" market briefings.*
- **Free analytical / commentary** (Money Stuff by Matt Levine, The Compound newsletter, Stocktwits *The Daily Rip*) — *context, wit, "why it matters."*
- **Paid stock-picking / options** (Motley Fool Stock Advisor & higher tiers, Stansberry, options-focused services) — *recommendations and alerts, not foundational education.*

### 3.2 Free vs. Paid — the critical distinction for an education app

- **Free daily digests + free commentary cover ~80% of a beginner's needs.** A stack of *The Daily Upside + Robinhood Snacks + Morning Brew + Money Stuff* is a complete, free way to stay informed. Beginners rarely *need* to pay.
- **Paid newsletters are mostly recommendation/alert engines, not teachers.** Motley Fool Stock Advisor (~$199/yr, frequently $99 intro), Epic (~$499), Stansberry, and options-alert rooms sell *what to buy / what to trade* and access to a community — they assume you'll learn by following along. The actual *education* component is secondary.
- **Implication:** our app competes less with paid pick services and more with the *free educational layer* — and our differentiator is **structured, interactive learning** (which neither podcasts nor digests provide).

### 3.3 Educational vs. market-commentary spectrum

```
Pure EDUCATION  ───────────────────────────────────────────►  Pure MARKET NEWS/COMMENTARY
 Option Alpha course   tastylive    InvestED   We Study Billionaires   Motley Fool Money
 Finimize explainers   Money Stuff (explainer-commentary)   Daily Upside   Morning Brew
                                   Robinhood Snacks / Daily Rip / Axios Markets / 1440
```

Most beginners actually live in the **middle** — "edutainment" that informs *and* entertains. Pure curriculum (Option Alpha, tastylive's course) is where they go when they decide to get serious. **An education app should bridge the two: the daily habit of a digest with the skill-building of a course.**

## 4. Key Trends & Takeaways

### 4.1 Audio is the "passive learning" on-ramp
Podcasts succeed because they attach learning to existing routines (commute, gym, chores) with near-zero friction. They build **motivation, vocabulary, and mental models** more than executable skill. Beginners over-index on *interview/mindset* shows (Chat With Traders, Top Traders Unplugged) because stories are more digestible than mechanics. **Lesson for us:** offer short audio explainers or "lesson recaps" for passive reinforcement between active sessions.

### 4.2 Daily-digest newsletters are habit machines
The 3–5 minute morning email (Morning Brew, Daily Upside, Snacks) is the single best habit-formation pattern in finance media: predictable time, tiny effort, a small reward (feeling informed + a laugh). 1440 reportedly added ~4.6M subscribers; The Daily Upside passed 1M; the category keeps compounding. **Lesson for us:** a **daily "market + lesson" push** at a consistent time (e.g. pre-market AM) can anchor a daily-open habit the same way these digests do.

### 4.3 The "edutainment" blend wins
The fastest-growing finance media — Ritholtz's Compound universe, Money Stuff, Robinhood Snacks, Odd Lots — all pair real substance with **personality, humor, and narrative**. Dry tutorials and long PDFs lose to conversational, story-led content. **Lesson for us:** wrap rigorous concepts (IV, theta, order books, payoff diagrams) in characters, scenarios, and a light voice. Tone is a feature.

### 4.4 Microlearning + gamification drive retention
- Microlearning research: short, single-concept modules (2–10 min) lift retention meaningfully (one cited study: ~20% better retention vs. long-form), and gamified financial programs report **~75% goal-completion vs. ~45%** without game mechanics, with **100–150% engagement lifts**.
- The **Duolingo model** (streaks, XP, levels, leaderboards, bite-sized daily lessons) is now the explicit template for finance learning apps. Duolingo runs ~50M daily / 135M+ monthly active users largely on habit mechanics. New finance entrants (e.g. *LearnFI* — "learn personal finance 5 minutes a day," AI tutor, streaks, minigames) and brokerages (**Zerodha** in India: quests, badges, trivia → reported +32% session time, +45% weekly app opens) are copying it directly.
- **Caution:** regulators (e.g. Ontario Securities Commission) warn that some gamification (points-for-trading, copy-trading) can *increase trading frequency* and herding — bad for users. **For an education app this is an advantage:** we can gamify *learning* (concepts mastered, streaks of lessons) rather than *trading*, capturing the engagement upside without the regulatory/behavioral downside.

### 4.5 Cross-format flywheels are the growth pattern
The strongest brands run **podcast ⇄ newsletter ⇄ video ⇄ community** loops (Ritholtz/Compound, The Investor's Podcast, tastylive, Motley Fool, Robinhood→Sherwood). Each format feeds the others and de-risks any single channel. **Lesson for us:** plan content so a single concept ships as an interactive lesson + a short audio recap + a digest blurb + a shareable card.

### 4.6 The unmet need: people want in-app education
Consumers say they want financial education but only **19%** get it from their apps; they ask specifically for **inflation-era strategies (71%)** and **accountability nudges (61%)**. The market is telling us to embed *teaching + nudging* directly into the product. **This is the whitespace our app targets.**

## 5. How an Education App Should Use This (Actionable)

1. **Own the daily habit.** Ship a consistent **daily bite-sized drop** (one concept + one market tie-in) at a fixed time, mirroring the morning-digest pattern that already conditions this audience.
2. **Two-speed content.** *Active* interactive lessons (the skill) + *passive* audio/visual recaps (the reinforcement) so learning fits both focused and idle time.
3. **Gamify learning, not trading.** Streaks, XP, levels, and "concepts mastered" — Duolingo mechanics aimed at knowledge, sidestepping the OSC's gamified-*trading* concerns.
4. **Edutainment voice.** Use characters, scenarios, humor, and narrative to teach IV, theta, payoff diagrams, and order-book mechanics — the tone that's winning in podcasts and newsletters.
5. **Bridge education ↔ news.** Tie each lesson to a current, real market story (the digest hook) so concepts feel relevant and timely, not abstract.
6. **Build a flywheel.** Repackage each lesson across formats (interactive → audio recap → email blurb → shareable card) and seed a light community layer for accountability nudges (which 61% explicitly want).
7. **Sequence for beginners.** Mirror the proven on-ramp: *story/motivation → plain-English basics → structured mechanics → applied practice.* (This is exactly the path beginners cobble together today across Chat With Traders → InvestED/Snacks → Option Alpha/tastylive.)
