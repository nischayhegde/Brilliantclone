# YouTube Trading & Investing Creators: The Beginner-Education Landscape (2025–2026)

> **Research brief for the product team.** This document maps the YouTube channels and video creators that amateur and beginner traders actually use to learn stock and options trading today. It covers who they are, how big they are, what they teach, how they make money, their credibility/criticism profile, and the format & business-model trends an education app should learn from.

---

## 1. Introduction

For most beginners in 2025–2026, the on-ramp to trading and investing is **YouTube, not a brokerage or a book**. The platform hosts everything from calm, index-fund personal-finance channels to adrenaline-fueled live day-trading streams. Understanding this ecosystem matters for an education product because these creators have already solved (or exploited) the hardest problem in our space: **keeping a financially anxious beginner engaged long enough to learn something.**

Three things stand out from the research:

1. **The audience is huge and segmented.** The top creators command 1M–5M+ subscribers each, but they serve very different intents — "help me get rich quick day trading" vs. "help me build wealth slowly" vs. "help me understand options mechanics."
2. **"Free YouTube" is almost always the top of a funnel.** The video is the lead magnet; the real business is a course, a Discord/Academy membership, a newsletter, or a broker affiliate relationship.
3. **Credibility varies wildly.** The same search results that recommend these channels also document FTC enforcement actions, "finfluencer" red flags, and the structural conflicts of the free→paid funnel. An education app can position itself as the *trustworthy* alternative.

> ⚠️ **A note on numbers:** Subscriber counts below are drawn from Social Blade, vidIQ, PLAYBOARD and similar trackers captured in **mid-2026**. YouTube rounds large counts (e.g., "2.06M"), and third-party trackers lag and disagree. Treat all figures as approximate and directional, not exact.

## 3. Channel-by-Channel Profiles

### A. Day-Trading Channels (highest hype, highest funnel intensity)

#### Warrior Trading — Ross Cameron (~2.06M)
- **Handle/URL:** `@daytradewarrior` · youtube.com/@daytradewarrior
- **Focus:** Momentum day trading of small-cap/penny stocks; the canonical "small account → big account" narrative ("$583.15 into over $10 million," with disclaimers that results are not typical).
- **Format:** Daily-recap style trade reviews, live trading, and structured education; ~5 uploads/week, 3,400+ videos since 2013.
- **Free vs. paid:** Free YouTube funnels to **Warrior Pro** courses, simulator, and chatroom/community (hundreds–thousands of dollars).
- **Credibility / criticism:** ⚠️ **Major flag.** In **April 2022 the FTC charged Warrior Trading and Ross Cameron** with deceptive earnings claims; the company settled for **$3M** and is **permanently barred from making unsubstantiated earnings claims.** The FTC has since returned **$2.9M+** to ~20,400 harmed customers. The FTC's core allegation: showcasing the founder's personal results implied customers would achieve similar success, while "the vast majority of customers actually lost money."
- **Why beginners watch:** Transparent-feeling recaps (wins *and* losses), a compelling rags-to-riches story, and concrete "here's the exact setup" teaching.

#### Ricky Gutierrez (~1.24M)
- **Handle/URL:** `@RickyGutierrez` (channel UC…CtQHA)
- **Focus:** Day trading, penny stocks, crypto, and real estate; a finance-meets-lifestyle blend.
- **Format:** Vlog-style "day in the life," live trading, and beginner education; 5,000+ videos.
- **Free vs. paid:** Free YouTube → **"Learn Plan Profit"** paid community/course.
- **Credibility / criticism:** Frequently criticized in trader forums for lifestyle marketing and lack of a verified, audited track record; broad topic-hopping (stocks/crypto/RE) is a common red-flag pattern. No regulatory action found, but treat earnings framing skeptically.
- **Why beginners watch:** Young, relatable, "you can do this too" energy; accessible entry point.

#### Humbled Trader — Shay Huang (~1.44M)
- **Handle/URL:** `@humbledtraderofficial`
- **Focus:** Day trading US stocks (plus an options track via a second coach, Adam); risk management and trading psychology over hype.
- **Format:** Education, vlogs, and some live content; ~1,050 videos since 2018.
- **Free vs. paid:** Free YouTube → **Humbled Trader Academy** (~**$990–$1,290/yr**, sometimes quoted to ~$1,870), bundling a stock course, options course, daily watchlists, weekly webinars, quarterly 1:1s, and a Discord.
- **Credibility / criticism:** Positioned explicitly as the **anti-Lamborghini** educator — "day trading is hard," openly discusses her ~$20K early losses. Generally regarded as legitimate. Criticisms: high single annual price with **no low-cost entry tier**, a **strict 10-day refund policy**, and concentration on a two-person coaching bench; some reviewers note core content can feel dated.
- **Why beginners watch:** Refreshing honesty, realistic expectations, and a risk-first philosophy ("it's not about how much you make, but how little you lose").

#### ClayTrader — "Clay" (~641K)
- **Handle/URL:** `@ClayTrader`
- **Focus:** Day trading and technical analysis for retail traders.
- **Format:** Live day-trading sessions, tutorials, and podcasts; ~4,900 videos.
- **Free vs. paid:** Free YouTube → **courses + "Inner Circle"** community membership.
- **Credibility / criticism:** "Regular guy" framing; breaks complex TA into digestible lessons. Standard funnel conflicts apply (course/community upsell), but no regulatory actions found.
- **Why beginners watch:** Plain-spoken, jargon-light explanations; approachable personality.

#### SMB Capital (~734K)
- **Handle/URL:** `@smbcapital`
- **Focus:** Professional/prop-desk day trading of equities and options; reading the tape, order flow, and "20 professional strategies."
- **Format:** Trader interviews, desk lectures, market recaps; ~2,500 videos, ~4/week. Education arm = **SMB U**.
- **Free vs. paid:** Free YouTube + free webinars → paid SMB U training programs and DNA mentorship.
- **Credibility / criticism:** One of the more **institutionally credible** brands (a real NYC proprietary trading firm). Still a paid-education funnel, but framed around professional skill development rather than get-rich-quick.
- **Why beginners watch:** Aspirational "this is how pros actually do it" content; perceived legitimacy.

#### TraderTV Live (~564K)
- **Handle/URL:** `@TraderTVLive` (Canada-based)
- **Focus:** Live day trading, primarily around the US market open.
- **Format:** All-day **live streams** with professional traders narrating real-time decisions; ~6,900 videos.
- **Free vs. paid:** Free live streams supported by sponsors/education partners and broker tie-ins.
- **Credibility / criticism:** Value is the live, unedited window into desks; the usual caveat is FOMO/imitation risk for beginners watching live P&L swings.
- **Why beginners watch:** Real-time exposure to multiple trading styles and how pros react to fast markets.

### B. Options-Focused Channels (the fastest-growing education niche)

#### InTheMoney — Adam (~458K)
- **Handle/URL:** `@InTheMoneyAdam`
- **Focus:** **Options** education and market commentary; known for breaking down complex options concepts simply. ~12 years creating content.
- **Format:** Explainer videos and commentary (relatively low upload count, high impact per video).
- **Free vs. paid:** Free YouTube → paid services (e.g., LEAPS management, "Autopilot" copy-trading) and community.
- **Credibility / criticism:** Widely cited as the most **beginner-friendly options explainer** on YouTube. Mix of education and product promotion; "educational only, not financial advice" disclaimers throughout. Watch for the promotional/copy-trade upsell.
- **Why beginners watch:** Turns intimidating options mechanics (calls, puts, spreads, Greeks) into clear, visual lessons.

#### tastylive / tastytrade (~333K, tastylive channel)
- **Handle/URL:** `@tastyliveshow` (network) — affiliated brokerage **tastytrade**
- **Focus:** **Options** trading from a **probabilistic / mechanics-based** philosophy; founded by options veterans (Tom Sosnoff et al.).
- **Format:** A **daily live financial network** (since 2011) — multiple recurring shows (e.g., *Options Trading Concepts Live*, *Mike and his Whiteboard*) distributed free across YouTube, web, apps, Roku, etc.
- **Free vs. paid:** **Content is free**; the business is the **tastytrade brokerage** (launched 2017). The show *is* the funnel to the broker.
- **Credibility / criticism:** Strong, consistent free educational catalog; clear "not investment advice" disclaimers. Conflict to note: content steers toward active options trading (and their brokerage), which generates order flow.
- **Why beginners watch:** A structured, repeatable framework (probabilities, mechanics over opinions) and a deep on-demand library.

#### projectfinance — Chris Butler (~423K)
- **Handle/URL:** `@projectfinance` (formerly "projectoption")
- **Focus:** **Options** education for beginner-to-intermediate traders; founder is a former tastylive employee.
- **Format:** Methodical tutorials and strategy mechanics; 30M+ views.
- **Free vs. paid:** Free YouTube → paid options courses; brokerage affiliate relationships.
- **Credibility / criticism:** Reputable, detail-oriented teaching; standard course/affiliate funnel.
- **Why beginners watch:** Clear, step-by-step strategy walk-throughs without heavy hype.

#### Option Alpha (large free library)
- **Handle/URL:** `@optionalpha`
- **Focus:** **Options** education + **automation**; "publish options education better than everyone else, then give it away free."
- **Format:** A **structured free curriculum** (beginner → intermediate → advanced courses: Options Basics, Entries & Exits, Expiration, Bullish/Neutral/Bearish strategies, Day Trading). 700+ videos, 20M+ views.
- **Free vs. paid:** Free education → paid **SaaS automation platform**.
- **Credibility / criticism:** Frequently ranked the **top channel for structured options learning**; the "free education, paid software" model has fewer earnings-claim conflicts than course/signal funnels.
- **Why beginners watch:** Genuinely **course-like structure** (modules, lessons, runtimes) — the closest analog to what an education app provides.

- **Free vs. paid:** Free YouTube → TradingwithRayner courses/community.
- **Credibility / criticism:** "No-hype," fundamentals-first reputation; emphasizes risk management. Among the more **trusted** retail TA educators.

#### Adam Khoo (~1.1M)

## 4. Key Trends & Takeaways

### Trend 1 — Short-form is for *discovery*; long-form is for *learning*
- **YouTube Shorts now reaches ~2B monthly users** and leads short-form platforms on engagement (~5.9%) and retention (~73%), and **finance Shorts earn ~10× the RPM** of comedy/lifestyle. Channels that pair **Shorts + long-form grow ~41% faster.**
- BUT short-form rarely converts directly to deep learning. A 721-Short, 22-creator analysis found **format and framing matter ~100× more than expertise depth** — *designed-for-shorts* content (e.g., The Plain Bagel, Patrick Boyle natively scripted) crushed *repurposed broadcast clips* (e.g., tastylive's chopped segments). **Humor and a strong 0–3s hook beat credentials.**
- **Takeaway for the app:** Use short-form (Shorts/TikTok/Reels) as a **top-of-funnel hook and concept-teaser** with one idea per clip; do the *real* teaching in structured, longer, sequenced content inside the app. Don't expect a viral Short to teach options — expect it to *recruit* the learner.

### Trend 2 — The "Free YouTube → Paid Discord/Course/Community" funnel is the dominant business model
- Nearly every channel here monetizes the same way: **free video = lead magnet → paid course / Academy / Discord / signals / brokerage affiliate.** The free content is engineered for reach; the money is downstream.
- Independent watchdogs document the **dark-pattern version** of this funnel: countdown timers/false scarcity, guaranteed-return language, cherry-picked "winning calls," signal groups with **no stop losses (74% in one audit) and unacknowledged losers (54%)**, and broker-affiliate kickbacks that reward **trade frequency over trade quality** ("you are the product").
- The **FTC vs. Warrior Trading** case ($3M settlement, $2.9M+ refunded) is the cautionary tale: showing a founder's real results to imply customer success is, by regulators' standard, **misleading.**
- **Takeaway for the app:** There's a clear **trust gap** in the market. An education product can differentiate by doing the opposite of the predatory funnel: **transparent outcomes, honest "most people lose" framing, simulated/paper trading, no signals, no false urgency, and pricing in the legitimate band ($200–$500 one-time, or a fair subscription) rather than $5,000 "mentorships."**

### Trend 3 — The formats that keep beginners engaged
From the live-stream and engagement research, the patterns that retain beginners:
1. **Predictable, repeatable structure** — e.g., *pre-market context → watchlist → setup walkthrough → execution/simulation → risk review → recap.* Viewers return for **rhythm**, and structured segments clip cleanly into reusable lessons.
2. **Show the losses, not just the wins.** "Highlight-reel bias" erodes trust; honesty about uncertainty/latency/mistakes compounds loyalty.
3. **Simulated / paper trades** to teach decision-making *without* inducing copycat risk — pause, annotate, review.
4. **Personality + light humor** lower the intimidation barrier on dry topics (the single biggest performance driver in short-form).
5. **Interactivity** — polls ("what would you do here?"), Q&A, and "homework" turn passive watching into active learning.

### Trend 4 — Segmented intent: meet beginners where they are
The audience splits cleanly into intents the app should treat as **distinct learning tracks**:
- **"Build wealth slowly"** → personal finance/investing (Graham Stephan, Andrei Jikh, Patrick Boyle). Largest, safest, lowest-risk audience.
- **"Understand options"** → InTheMoney, tastylive, projectfinance, Option Alpha. Fast-growing; mechanics-heavy; great for structured curricula.
- **"Learn to day trade / TA"** → Warrior, Humbled Trader, ClayTrader, SMB, Rayner Teo, The Trading Channel. Highest engagement *and* highest risk/hype.

### Trend 5 — Gamification works, but needs guardrails
- Gamification can lift **90-day fintech retention by 30–40%** via streaks (loss aversion), variable rewards (dopamine loops), and leaderboards (social/FOMO).
- The Robinhood cautionary lesson: the *same* mechanics that drive adoption can encourage **overconfidence, impulsivity, and overtrading** if pointed at speculation rather than learning.
- **Takeaway for the app:** Gamify **learning milestones, simulated-trade discipline, and risk-management habits** — *not* real-money trade frequency. Align incentives with education (Brilliant/Duolingo-style streaks on *lessons completed and concepts mastered*), and add intentional friction + risk disclosures before high-stakes simulated decisions.

## 5. What an Education App Should Steal (and Avoid)

**Steal:**
- **Option Alpha's structured free curriculum** model (modules → lessons → runtimes) — it's the closest YouTube analog to in-app courses and proves beginners *will* follow a sequenced syllabus.
- **InTheMoney's "make the scary thing simple" explainer craft** for options (visual, one-concept-at-a-time).
- **Humbled Trader / Patrick Boyle's honesty positioning** ("this is hard; most people lose") as a brand differentiator and a trust moat.
- **Live-stream structure** (context → setup → simulated execution → risk review → recap) as a **template for interactive lessons** and "guided practice" sessions.
- **Short-form hooks** to drive top-of-funnel acquisition, then convert to structured in-app learning.
- **Gamified streaks/badges** tied to *learning and simulated practice.*

**Avoid:**
- Earnings/outcome promises (FTC-actionable and trust-destroying).
- Signals, copy-trading, and "VIP Discord" upsells that reward trade frequency.
- False scarcity, countdown timers, lifestyle/Lamborghini marketing.
- Gamification that nudges real-money overtrading.
